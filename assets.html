Operational assets

Company, Device, Vehicle, and Subscription Flow

Company

The ownership boundary for operational assets. Companies form a root, agency, and dealer hierarchy through parentId, treeId, and type flags such as isSystem, isAgency, and isDealer.

company.type.ts:12 / company-create-update-dialog.component.ts:119

Device

The physical tracker or camera-capable unit. It has a current companyId, can be transferred between companies, and may later be bound into a subscription and vehicle.

device.type.ts:37 / device.api.service.ts:122

Vehicle

The logical vehicle record inside a company. It is created with plate, type, group, speed, and registry metadata. It becomes connected to a device through subscription assignment, not through a standalone device-to-vehicle endpoint in the reviewed frontend.

vehicle.type.ts:25 / vehicle.api.service.ts:46

Subscription

The binding record. It carries companyId, subscriptionTypeId, deviceId, vehicleId, and intervals. Its assign and unassign endpoints change the device or vehicle link while keeping the company scope.

subscription.type.ts:3 / subscription.api.service.ts:108

State model

How an asset becomes usable

Root inventory

Unassigned root stock is loaded from /devices/free.

Company stock

The device is moved to a company with swap-single, swap-device-back, or asset transfer.

Free in company

Assignment dialogs load free same-company devices and vehicles before creating links.

Subscription bound

A subscription attaches one device and one vehicle to a subscription type.

Active unit

activeDate represents the full subscription plus vehicle state; assignDate represents subscription-only assignment.

Moving devices between companies

Root inventory transfer

The company device inventory tab opens the root transfer dialog. It loads source devices from getFreeRootDevices(), target-company free devices from getFreeDevices(companyId), moves selected root devices with swapDeviceSingle(), and returns devices to root with swapDeviceBack().

company-detail-device-inventory.component.ts:193 / device-root-transfer-dialog.component.ts:239

Parent and child company swap

The company device tab opens the device swap dialog. In a two-tier tree, the source list is the parent company or root; in a three-tier tree, the dialog parses treeId and lets devices move from tier 1 or tier 2 into the target tier 3 company. Returning uses the matching swapDeviceBack() path.

device-swap-dialog.component.ts:57 / device-swap-dialog.component.ts:391 / device-swap-dialog.component.ts:467

Direct asset move

Selected devices can be sent to another non-system company through /devices/swap-assets-to-company. The target picker hides the current company and system company. This flow passes selected device ids directly, so backend validation decides whether an already-linked device may move.

company-detail-device.component.ts:277 / device-swap-asset-dialog.component.ts:177

Auto move and provision

The auto swap dialog sends fromCompanyId, toCompanyId, and per-device items containing deviceId, subscriptionTypeId, and plateNumber. This is the one-step move where the backend can also create or bind the vehicle/subscription relationship.

Binding devices, vehicles, and subscriptions

Create the vehicle in the company first

Vehicle create and update are company-scoped. The form submits companyId with plate, vehicle type, group, max speed, and optional metadata.

vehicle-create-update-dialog.component.ts:312 / vehicle.api.service.ts:46

Create or update the subscription

Subscription create loads free devices and free vehicles for the same companyId. Create requires companyId, subscriptionTypeId, deviceId, and vehicleId. Update changes type, device, vehicle, intervals, and description, but not the company.

subscription-create-update-dialog.component.ts:214 / subscription-create-update-dialog.component.ts:344 / subscription.type.ts:14

Attach or detach from the subscriptions tab

If a subscription is missing a device or vehicle, the table opens assignment dialogs. Device assignment loads getFreeDevices(companyId) and posts to /subscriptions/assign-device. Vehicle assignment loads getFreeVehicles(companyId) and posts to /subscriptions/assign-vehicle. Removal posts to the subscription-specific unassign endpoints.

Assign selected devices from the devices tab

The device assign dialog filters selected devices to !device.isAssigned, loads free vehicles in the same company, and sends swapDeviceAuto() with identical fromCompanyId and toCompanyId. Each device item selects a subscription type and either an existing free vehicleId or a new plateNumber.

Rules

Operational rules to keep straight

Company scope comes first

Devices, vehicles, and subscriptions are all filtered by companyId before assignment.

Subscription is the connector

The reviewed frontend does not expose a simple direct device-to-vehicle assignment endpoint. The relationship is created through subscriptions or swap-auto.

Free lists drive assignment

Assignment forms load /devices/free-company and /vehicles/free, preventing the user from selecting resources already linked elsewhere.

Move and bind are separate

swap-single, swap-device-back, and asset transfer change company ownership. Subscription assign and unassign change the binding.

Assigned is not always active

For devices, assignDate means subscription only; activeDate means subscription plus vehicle.

Backend is final authority

The frontend shapes choices and calls the correct endpoints, but business conflicts such as moving a linked asset are enforced by backend responses.

Key point: a device moves between companies as an owned asset, then becomes useful to tracking through a subscription that connects it with a vehicle. Removing a device or vehicle from a subscription frees the link; it does not move the asset to another company.
